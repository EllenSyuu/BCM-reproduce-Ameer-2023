## Survey / census data (registration required, free)

**IMPORTANT for accurate reproduction**: use the paper's own bibliography (Appendix
A.1 + References, transcribed below) as the authoritative list of exactly which
survey rounds/vintages were used, NOT the UIS `ROFST.H` public-API footnotes we
initially looked at as a proxy. Checked directly: `ROFST.H`'s footnotes are UIS's
live, continuously-updated indicator and already include rounds that postdate the
paper (e.g. "Angola DHS 2023-24", "Benin MICS 2021-22" — the paper is from August
2022 with a 2000-2022 data window). Using that footnote catalog as a request list
would pull in survey rounds the paper's authors never used and could diverge from
their exact per-country-year selections. It remains useful only as a loose
cross-check once real data is in hand, not as the acquisition target.

- **DHS (Demographic and Health Surveys)** — https://dhsprogram.com/
  Paper's exact citation: ICF, 2000-2018 vintage. Requires a free account + stating
  research purpose; approval typically fast. Need: individual recode / household
  member recode files, school attendance variables, survey design/weight variables,
  cluster IDs (for Jackknife SE). Restrict extract requests to survey rounds falling
  in 2000-2018 to match the paper's vintage.
- **MICS (Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys)** — https://mics.unicef.org/
  Paper's exact citation: UNICEF, 1999-2019 vintage. Free registration. Need
  household/member-level datasets with education module. Restrict to rounds in
  1999-2019.
- **IPUMS International** — https://international.ipums.org/international/
  Paper's exact citation: Minnesota Population Center, Version 7.3 (2020). Free
  registration, extracts capped at 1M records per the paper's own constraint.
  Census samples with school attendance/completion variables.

## Country-specific household surveys (16 countries, Appendix A.1)
The paper sourced all 16 of these through the **World Inequality Database on
Education (WIDE)** — https://www.education-inequalities.org/, a UNESCO-affiliated
portal that aggregates national household survey microdata — rather than registering
separately with each country's statistics office. "WIDE" is that aggregator's name,
not a distinct data category to hunt down. Worth checking WIDE first for any of the
16 before going to the original national agency listed below.

Collectively only ~1.5% of the paper's data (Table 1's "Other" category, spread
across 16 countries) — lower priority than DHS/MICS/IPUMS, which alone cover most
of the 135 countries with survey data. Defer until the DHS/MICS/IPUMS pipeline works.

Exact years below are the paper's own bibliography citations (References section) —
use these to pin down the correct vintage/round per country, not whatever is
currently latest on each agency's portal.

**Decision 2026-09-29:** Papua New Guinea (HIES 2009) and Colombia (ECV 2019) are
treated as unavailable and excluded from the reproduction — no usable microdata
or WIDE extract could be found (Colombia has only the education module without
the person file). The other 14 WIDE sources are used. Documented deviation:
"Other" loses 2 of 16 country-surveys (a small part of the 1.5% "Other" share).

**Decision 2026-09-29 (WIDE, all 14):** the WIDE files we have are published
education-level aggregates, not single-age, so they cannot enter Eq. 2. The
single-age tables / harmonized microdata behind WIDE are not obtainable (internal
constraints). The "Other" series is therefore **left out of the model for now**
(documented deviation, ~1.5% of the paper's observations).
`harmonize_wide.py` output (`wide_oosc_observations.csv`, 348 level rows) is kept
so it can be added later — either via single-age data if it becomes available,
or via a level-aggregated likelihood term.

## Acquisition scripts
See `D. Code/D.1. Paper 1 - OOSC Cohort Model (4.1.4)/Python/fetch_public_data.py` for
WPP/Eurostat/SingStat pulls (population side only — these are unblocked and should
be run first). DHS/MICS/IPUMS require manual extract requests through their web
portals before any script can download microdata; stubs for the post-download
harmonization step will live alongside the other Python modules once files are in
`A. Data/A.1. Raw Sources/3. Survey Data/` (read with `pyreadstat` for the
.dta/.sav survey files):
- `1. MICS (1999-2019)/` — MICS_Datasets.zip received and verified 2026-09-14
  (247 country-round datasets, proper SPSS .sav files). Processed by
  `harmonize_mics.py`: 197 national surveys usable; subnational surveys excluded;
  8 skipped (Cuba 2006/2010/2014 and Argentina 2011 never ask current-year
  attendance; Burundi 2000 and Lesotho 2000 have no weight in hl.sav; Botswana
  2000 is unlabelled; Guinea-Bissau 2010 ships one merged file). Per-survey
  status in `A. Data/A.2. Processed/mics_file_inventory.csv`.
- `2. DHS (2000-2018, pending project approval)/` — 182 household-member recode
  (PR, flat ASCII) files received 2026-09-29: every standard-DHS PR file dated
  2000-2018 in the approved download list. 3 (BDPR41, GAPR41, VNPR41) lack hv121
  and are skipped. Processed by `harmonize_dhs.py`; per-file status in
  `A. Data/A.2. Processed/dhs_file_inventory.csv`.
- `3. IPUMS International (v7.3 census)/` — `ipumsi_00003.csv.gz` received
  2026-09-28 (CSV with header: COUNTRY, YEAR, SAMPLE, SERIAL, HHWT, GQ, PERNUM,
  PERWT, AGE, SEX, SCHOOL, EDATTAIN(D), YRSCHOOL, ...). Chosen over the other
  downloads: 00001/00002 are identical fixed-width `.dat` files and 00004 is a
  fixed-width variant, all unreadable without their DDI `.xml` codebooks, which
  were not downloaded. Processed by `harmonize_ipums_census.py`; per-sample
  coverage in `A. Data/A.2. Processed/ipums_sample_inventory.csv`.
